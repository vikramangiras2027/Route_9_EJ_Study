1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
====================================================================================================
 1. Measured traffic now drives the corridor picture: MassDOT segment AADT covers all 17.7 km of Route 9 in Worcester + Shrewsbury (98% of length is 2023-2025 data). All 8 on-route count stations got an AADT (4 via LRS station ID and milepost, 4 by spatial match); the Statn_Num join named in the brief matched none, because that field is empty.
 2. Loading ranges 13,461-45,937 veh/day (length-weighted mean 28,373); 44% of the corridor carries >= 30k and it rises from Worcester (mean 20,646) to Shrewsbury (mean 40,615). The old single value (28,112, M3002W) is +5.5% against the MassDOT segment at that milepost but misstates both ends.
 3. The 75% westbound skew (20,962 WB vs 7,148 EB) is one station's count, not a corridor property.
 4. Who lives near: about 28,786 EJ-designated residents (13%) live within 300 m, 47,144 (22%) within 500 m and 81,149 (37%) within 1 km of Route 9; households within 500 m ~17,275 (18% of all in the two towns).
 5. The busiest stretches are not beside the lowest-income neighbourhoods: ~8,633 EJ residents / ~3,652 households live within 500 m of segments carrying >= 30k veh/day (median income $115,291), mostly at the Shrewsbury end; in Worcester only 957 EJ residents do, while its low-income corridor neighbourhoods sit beside 14k-28k segments.
 6. Worcester (primary): household-weighted median income is $56,458 in the corridor vs $82,432 in the rest of the city; 46% vs 31% of households earn under $50k.
 7. Within Worcester, tract income rises with distance from Route 9 (rho = +0.48, tract-bootstrap 95% CI [+0.17, +0.72], n = 44 tracts); with town fixed effects the slope is +$11,243/km (CI +$6,609 to +$15,876). Moran's I ~0.73, so intervals use cluster / tract bootstrap.
 8. EJ designation: 97% of Worcester corridor block groups vs 88% elsewhere in the city (odds ratio 4.6, p=0.058); high everywhere, with only a suggestive (not conclusive) edge near the road.
 9. Shrewsbury is a sensitivity case: 11 corridor block groups in 5 tracts, tract median income $134,585; pooling both towns (tract rho +0.41) vs Worcester alone (+0.48) changes the gradient little, but adds the highest-volume stretch with comparatively few nearby residents.
10. Without any traffic term, 27 corridor block groups (34,339 EJ residents; 27 in Worcester) rank in the top vulnerability quintile (rho with distance -0.45); the legacy CBI's distance link (-0.75) is partly built in.
11. PM2.5 is regional only: 2025 mean 5.65 ug/m3 (below the 9 federal annual standard, above the WHO 5 guideline), no day above the federal 24-h standard; monitors 23 km apart move together (rho 0.95); there is no roadside monitor on Route 9.
12. Results are neighbourhood-scale associations; near-road exposure is modelled, populations are interpolated, and nothing here shows individual exposure, health effects or causation.

Recommended next steps for the advisory group:
  a. Commission near-road PM2.5 / NO2 (or UFP) monitoring on the >= 30k stretches and beside priority block groups; ambient county monitors cannot answer the corridor question.
  b. Ask MassDOT for classification counts with truck share and directional volumes at M3002W-class stations to verify the westbound skew and refresh older (pre-2023) segments.
  c. Treat Worcester as the primary equity case; review the priority block-group list (Figure 3, tract table) with neighbourhood groups before any targeting decision.
  d. Fill the population gap (decennial / ACS block-group population for non-EJ block groups) so near-road counts cover everyone, not only EJ-designated residents.
  e. Pair this analysis with tract health outcomes (e.g., asthma ED visits) if the group wants evidence on effects rather than proximity.
  f. Re-run annually (fetch_massdot_traffic_aadt.py --refresh) as MassDOT updates AADT vintages.

====================================================================================================
8. LIMITATIONS
====================================================================================================
- Traffic exposure at block groups is modelled (distance decay from segment AADT, not measured). AADT is annual average, both directions, with no truck share, grade, lane geometry or wind; vintages are mostly 2024-2025 but some segments are older (see Section 3).
- The corridor centreline is the Census TIGER 'State Rte 9' line; MassDOT segments are matched to it within a few metres. Only Route 9 is modelled: I-290, I-190, I-495 and cross streets are not, so block groups near them are not marked as exposed.
- Count-station IDs are inconsistent (blank and repeated numbers, M3002W does not equal STATION_NUMBER 3002); stations are used for validation and context, segment AADT drives the model.
- The WB 75% skew belongs to one station and is not generalised.
- Population: EJ_POLY gives residents only for the 149 EJ-designated block groups; non-EJ block-group populations are unknown (EJ residents undercount, especially Shrewsbury). Households are ACS tract households shared across block groups (an allocation, not a count).
- Block-group incomes repeat tract ACS estimates (some with CV > 30%); household-weighted pooled medians come from bracket shares and are approximate. EJ-table incomes are an older ACS vintage.
- Near-road residents are interpolated by area (uniform density). Results are ecological associations at neighbourhood scale, not individual exposure, health effects or causation.
- Moran's I is high (about 0.7 at block-group level), so naive p-values are overstated; tract / cluster bootstrap intervals are reported instead.
- PM2.5 is regional context from two county monitors (Worcester Summer St, Uxbridge) and one year; it cannot be localised to Route 9 or used to rank block groups. Nearest-monitor columns in the master table are labels, not exposure.
- CBI correlates with distance partly by construction (its traffic term is a function of distance); use vulnerability_index for traffic-independent statements.
