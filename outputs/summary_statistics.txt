6. KEY QUANTITATIVE FINDINGS (policy brief)
====================================================================================================
1. Traffic loading. Route 9 at M3002W carries 28,112 vehicles/day, 74.6% westbound (20,962 WB vs 7,148 EB; 2.9:1). An estimated 47,144 EJ-designated residents (21.7% of all 217,466 EJ residents in the two towns) live within 500 m and 81,149 (37.3%) within 1 km of the centreline.

2. EJ designation is high everywhere but highest on the corridor. 90% of all block groups in Worcester and Shrewsbury are EJ-designated (vs 36% in the rest of the county and 51% statewide), and 96% of corridor block groups (centroid <= 1.5 km) vs 84% elsewhere in the two towns (odds ratio 5.1, p=0.009). EJ block groups sit a median 1,397 m from Route 9 vs 2,465 m for non-EJ (rho = -0.19, cluster-bootstrap 95% CI [-0.32, -0.06]). The effect is modest: at tract level the association is weaker (rho = -0.22, p=0.103).

3. Income gradient. Median household income tends to rise with distance from Route 9: Spearman rho = +0.47 (p<0.001; tract-cluster bootstrap 95% CI [0.20, 0.66]); tract-level rho = +0.41. That is +$10,651 in tract median income per km from the road (citywide pooled OLS). Spatial clustering is strong (Moran's I = 0.73), which shrinks the effective sample size.

   Town-confound check: within Worcester alone the gradient is rho = +0.57 (p<0.001, n=144, cluster CI [0.29, 0.74]; +$10,926/km); within Shrewsbury alone rho = +0.64 (p=0.002, n=20, CI [0.02, 0.88]). The Worcester-only result shows the gradient is not merely the Worcester-vs-Shrewsbury town divide.

4. Corridor gap. Median tract income is $52,011 in the corridor vs $84,407 elsewhere in the two towns (-$32,396); the corridor median equals 50% of the statewide median ($103,960). Households earning under $50k: 44.6% in the corridor vs 31.3% elsewhere. Worcester+Shrewsbury tracts overall have a median of $81,073 vs $104,236 for the rest of Worcester County.

5. Cumulative burden. The traffic-independent vulnerability score (low income + EJ criteria met) also rises toward Route 9 (rho with distance = -0.45, p<0.001). 33 corridor block groups (42,805 EJ residents, 19.7% of the study EJ population) are in the top quintile of the cumulative burden index.

6. PM2.5. Mean 5.65 ug/m3, peak 33.5. The federal 24-h standard (35) was exceeded on 0 of 705 site-days (98th percentile 18.2); the annual mean is below the 9 ug/m3 federal annual standard but above the WHO 5 ug/m3 guideline; 23 site-days (3.3%, 14 distinct dates) exceeded the WHO 24-h guideline of 15, 100% of them in June-August. The two monitors correlate at rho = 0.95, so the episodes are regional, not corridor-specific.

LIMITATIONS
- One AADT station: block-group traffic is a distance-decay model (300 m e-folding), not a measurement; it ignores lane geometry, truck share, grade and wind.
- PM2.5 comes from two monitors (Worcester Summer St, Uxbridge) and cannot differentiate block groups; it is regional context, not a corridor-specific measurement.
- EJ_POLY lists only EJ-designated block groups. Non-EJ block groups were identified from Census geometry, so demographics (population, % minority, % LEP) are unavailable for them.
- ACS 2024 tract incomes are applied to block groups (some tracts have wide margins of error: see acs_income_reliable); EJ-table incomes are an older ACS vintage.
- Population near Route 9 is interpolated by area (uniform-density assumption). Results are ecological associations, not individual exposure or health effects.
- One year of PM2.5 data cannot establish NAAQS compliance, which uses 3-year design values.
